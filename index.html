The usual suspects

Patterns, not promises. Tap the one that sounds familiar.

Accountant or bookkeeper

The same client details typed into practice software, the tax package and the portal. Records chased by email with no record of who has replied. January spent asking for the same documents in the same order. A fix looks like: one intake that fills all three systems, chasing that runs itself until the document arrives, and a list of who still owes what.

Marketing agency

You generate the leads. They sit in a spreadsheet or an inbox until someone gets round to them, and by then they are cold. The client blames lead quality. Reporting eats the first week of every month. A fix looks like: leads answered inside five minutes without anyone touching them, and client reports that assemble themselves overnight from the ad accounts.

Recruitment

Candidates re-keyed between job boards, the ATS and the CRM. Follow-ups that depend on someone remembering. CVs reformatted by hand before they go to clients. A fix looks like: one candidate record everywhere at once, follow-up that fires itself, and CVs formatted in seconds in your house style.

Trades and construction

Quotes sent and never chased. Job sheets written on paper and typed into accounts later. Variations done on site and never billed. A fix looks like: quotes that chase themselves, job sheets filled in on a phone that land straight in the accounts, and every variation logged and invoiced.

Clinic

No-shows nobody follows up. Recalls that were supposed to run and did not. Enquiries out of hours answered the next working day, if at all. A fix looks like: reminders that arrive when people actually read them, recalls that run on schedule, and out-of-hours enquiries answered the moment they come in.

Manufacturing and wholesale

A stock spreadsheet that disagrees with the system. Orders entered twice, once for the customer and once for production. Delivery notes that never quite match the invoice. A fix looks like: one version of the stock truth, one order entry, and paperwork that agrees with itself.

Not on the list? It is still one of three things: something typed twice, something that falls between two systems, or something nobody follows up.
